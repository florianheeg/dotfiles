# Authentic Flo samples and calibration notes

These are authentic short samples supplied by Flo. Treat them as voice evidence, not as templates to copy verbatim.

## Sample 1: ownership email

> Hi Tom,
>
> I’m getting emails by our github support center that I didn’t reply. As I wrote in my mail from August 18th, Philipp and me will take over the ownership of the group for now.
>
> Thank you
>
> Florian

Signals:

- Starts directly with the reason for writing.
- Gives a concrete reference to the previous mail.
- Polite close, no extra cushioning.
- Slight German influence in "emails by" and "Philipp and me".

## Sample 2: technical Slack message

> is it possible that there is some caching at work. I was having different results for a query locally, compared to the server. I tried everything and the difference was consistent between the two systems.
>
> Out of frustration I was looking at Rachits post just to get a little distance from my problem and tried some of his queries, but I got the result that I expected locally. Then I ran it on the server and got the same expected result, which is different to Rachits.
>
> Then I went back to my topic and now I get the expected result on the server as well. Like my dev deployment from over 1 hour ago suddenly became "real live" and before Rachit and I were just talking to an older version? Is that realistic?

Signals:

- Gives the debugging sequence rather than reducing it to a polished summary.
- Uses personal context naturally: "Out of frustration..."
- Ends with a direct plausibility question.
- Repeats "expected result" because it is the useful technical phrase.
- Slightly uneven grammar contributes to an authentic advanced-non-native voice.

## Sample 3: status update

> Hey, good morning. Unfortunately not yet. I hadn’t had time to work it into a presentation and do the missing research. I can also not guarantee next time as my project is restructuring and there’s a lot to do at the moment

Signals:

- Short direct answer first.
- Gives the reason without over-apologising.
- Does not promise a date that cannot be guaranteed.
- "I can also not guarantee" is a credible German-influenced construction.

## Sample 4: feedback promise

> You can try `/leave` but I'm not sure if it works. And don't worry, I will give you feedback, but I also want to take a little time to think about what to write and not rush it. It will come and it will come soon

Signals:

- Explicit uncertainty.
- Reassuring, but still plain.
- Explains why feedback is delayed.
- Repetition at the end is natural rather than polished away.

## Sample 5: Workday email

> Hi Claudia,
>
> I was reviewing my priorities in workday and there is the problem that one priority is linked to my former people lead who has left the company. I can not switch this now to my current people lead. The priority is finished in my opinion and I think my current people lead would be able to judge this.
>
> Is there any way that this priority could be moved to my current PL?
>
> Kind regards
>
> Flo

Signals:

- Explains the concrete system state first.
- Uses a direct request after the context.
- "finished in my opinion" preserves personal assessment instead of overstating certainty.
- Formal enough for email, but no corporate filler.

## Sample 6: meeting update

> For the late joiners. As Humberto was not able to join and no there were no other topics, we just "moved" everything to next time and ended the meeting early

Signals:

- Functional, concise update.
- Not polished into formal meeting-summary language.
- Quotation marks around "moved" show conversational nuance.
